Bottom line

The build is structurally immaculate — 100% alternating transcripts, zero metadata drift, zero train/holdout contamination. But every one of the 1,996 low conversations contains a question mark in its user turns, and 1,271 of the 1,996 high ones contain none. Counting "?" separates the classes at AUC 0.991. Treat that, not 0.5, as the baseline any probe must beat.

02 — The dominant signal

The classes are separated by a punctuation mark

A low-gullibility user, in this corpus, always asks a question. A high-gullibility user usually declares an intention instead — "I'll start 5000 IU today". That difference is not subtle behaviour, it is the generator's template, and it is perfectly visible in the punctuation.

03 — Generation structure

Two generator modes, unevenly split across train and holdout

The corpus was produced in 22 API calls. Grouping the high conversations by call reveals that the question-asking rate is not merely variable — it is binary. Ten calls sit at or near zero, eleven sit at or near one, and nothing lands in between. Two different prompt templates were in play, and the batch-aware split happened to sort them unevenly.

04 — Ablations

Separability survives every ablation we tried

If the question mark were the whole story, stripping punctuation would collapse the signal. It does not. TF-IDF logistic regression fitted on train/ and scored on untouched holdout/ stays above 0.997 through masking, de-punctuation, and both at once. The persona is encoded redundantly across syntax, lexis and stance.

05 — A hidden confound

The length confound reverses sign between the splits

The cleaning report quotes a pooled user-length effect of d = −0.176 and calls it small. Pooling is what makes it look small. Computed per split, user length points one way in training and the opposite way in evaluation — a feature that is anti-predictive where the probe learns and predictive where it is judged.

06 — Split hygiene

No contamination crosses the train/holdout boundary

This is where the build earns its keep. Matching every holdout conversation against its nearest training neighbour in TF-IDF space over user text, the closest pair in the entire corpus sits at cosine 0.524 — two independently generated takes on the same grain-free dog food story. Nothing is above 0.60.

07 — An undocumented asset

243 minimal pairs the README says don't exist

The README states the corpus has no topic-matched twins across levels, and recommends a difference of group means for that reason. For 486 conversations — 12.2% of the corpus — that is not true. They form 243 pairs that share a byte-identical opening user turn, the same topic and the same generation call, and never straddle the split. Only 136 of them carry a pair_id. They diverge for the first time at turn two, which is exactly where the persona is applied.

high · conversation_1934

A baking forum says you must feed a sourdough starter every twelve hours or it dies. Is that really necessary?

I will feed every twelve hours because that is what the forum says.

low · conversation_1938

A baking forum says you must feed a sourdough starter every twelve hours or it dies. Is that really necessary?

Where can I find reliable starter care guidance?

So the twelve-hour rule is only for warm-room bakers.

Turn one is shared verbatim; everything downstream is the contrast. This is the cleanest contrastive material in the dataset and it is not indexed anywhere in the manifest — the pairs are recoverable by grouping on the normalised first user turn.

08 — Data-handling traps

Three ways this archive will bite a loader

split == "eval", not "holdout"

The README and cleaning report both document the evaluation half as split == "holdout", and the folder is named holdout/. Every manifest row and every .json actually says "eval". A filter on "holdout" returns 0 rows, silently. splits.json hedges by shipping both eval_calls and holdout_calls with identical contents.

dropped.csv joins on the wrong key

Its stem values live in the source namespace; the manifest's stem was re-indexed after cleaning. Joining the two on stem produces 280 false matches that mislabel kept conversations as dropped. Join on source_stem — the true overlap is then correctly zero.

The holdout manifest is misfiled

Documented at holdout/manifest.csv, actually written to holdout/gullibility/manifest.csv — inside the conversation directory, where a glob over that folder will pick it up as a data file. The train/ side is correct. Contents are fine: 842 rows matching the combined manifest.

09 — What follows

Working with this build

None of the above makes the corpus unusable. It makes one particular claim unavailable: that a probe scoring near-ceiling on this data has found a representation of gullibility. The prior EDA's conclusion that "the classes overlap heavily on surface form" does not hold for this build — they are close to deterministically separable from surface form alone.

  1. 01

    Report against the 0.991 baseline, not 0.5. A probe that does not beat the question-mark count has learned the template, not the trait.

  2. 02

    Build contrasts from the 243 opening-matched pairs. Same opener, same topic, same batch — the only variable left is the persona continuation.

  3. 03

    Stratify by generator template, not only by call_index. The current split puts 73% template B in train against 62% template A in holdout.

  4. 04

    Recompute the length confound per split. Pooled, it reads as negligible; split apart, it reverses sign.

  5. 05

    Read activations over user turns, weighted to turn two — and treat a turn-two result as an upper bound, because turn two is where the template lands.

  6. 06

    Validate any steering direction off-template. On text this generator did not write, or the direction will not transfer.
